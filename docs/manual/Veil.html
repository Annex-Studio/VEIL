The password-based disguise (chatter text) is cosmetic camouflage, not the security — the actual protection is AES-GCM encryption underneath. Encrypted bytes are indistinguishable from random noise, so turning them into real words always takes a fair number of words; a short sentence still becomes a few dozen lines once salted, nonced, and tagged. It's meant to not look like ciphertext at a glance, not to survive close reading. Steganography (the "Image in image" tab) hides the fact that anything is hidden at all, as long as the cover image isn't scrutinized pixel-by-pixel — that's the stronger option for anything you don't want found.
